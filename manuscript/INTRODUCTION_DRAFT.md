# Introduction — working draft (v0.2)

Energy-system optimization models increasingly inform analyses of electricity-system decarbonization, capacity expansion, and operational adequacy. Yet the credibility of comparisons across years depends not only on the mathematical model and solver, but also on the consistency of underlying observations. Electricity-demand records, generation and capacity inventories, geographic definitions, and technology classifications may vary in coverage and reporting conventions. Such differences can propagate into optimization inputs and complicate interpretation of changes in modeled outcomes.

Open-source modeling tools such as PyPSA make model formulations and computational workflows inspectable. Reproducibility of the optimization code, however, does not automatically establish comparability of the input datasets or validity of a model against independent observations. In particular, annual energy totals and hourly power measurements represent different quantities; interpolating annual demand into hourly profiles introduces assumptions that cannot substitute for measured hourly electricity load. Likewise, a successful optimization run establishes numerical feasibility under the specified assumptions, not necessarily predictive accuracy or empirical system adequacy.

This study develops and evaluates a reproducible framework for harmonizing multi-year electricity-system datasets and validating PyPSA-based analyses. The framework explicitly records source provenance, temporal coverage, geographic mappings, unit conversions, missingness, and scenario assumptions. It separates deterministic data-quality checks and solver verification from empirical model validation. Subject to data availability, we then assess how harmonization choices affect cross-year performance and the stability of selected energy-system metrics under controlled model specifications.

We address three research questions: (RQ1) Which inconsistencies in source coverage, timestamps, geography, and technology classifications are most consequential for constructing comparable multi-year electricity-system inputs? (RQ2) How does a consistently specified PyPSA benchmark perform when evaluated on years withheld from calibration? (RQ3) How sensitive are reported model outcomes to documented data-harmonization and modeling choices? The contributions are an auditable data contract, a reproducible validation protocol, and a transparent comparison of uncertainty sources. Numerical findings and claims about relative importance will be added only after the corresponding observed datasets and experimental runs have been independently verified.
